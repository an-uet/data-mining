Largest error #2: 2016-12-22 03:00 actual 149, predicted 396, error -247 - sudden clean-up: PM2.5 fell -263 ug/m3 within one hour (training 95th percentile of falls: -43); the forecast stays close to the previous high level; limited warning in the history: the jump (263) is 16.5x the 6-hour standard deviation (16) preceding it; the wind was calm (0.4 m/s, below the training median 1.4), so a ventilation-driven clean-up is not visible in the local wind data (possible local plume change or measurement artefact); PM10 equals PM2.5(t) exactly in this hour (a data-quality quirk present in 23 % of the training hours): the coarse-particle reading adds no independent information here
Largest error #3: 2017-02-15 14:00 actual 3, predicted 226, error -223 - sudden clean-up: PM2.5 fell -226 ug/m3 within one hour (training 95th percentile of falls: -43); the forecast stays close to the previous high level; limited warning in the history: the jump (226) is 16.9x the 6-hour standard deviation (13) preceding it; the wind was calm (0.6 m/s, below the training median 1.4), so a ventilation-driven clean-up is not visible in the local wind data (possible local plume change or measurement artefact); PM10 equals PM2.5(t) exactly in this hour (a data-quality quirk present in 23 % of the training hours): the coarse-particle reading adds no independent information here
Shared failure pattern: all 3 largest errors are abrupt one-hour changes (|change| 226-271 ug/m3, 5.3-8.2x the training 95th percentile of changes in that direction); in each, the forecast stays within 3-21 ug/m3 of the last observed PM2.5(t), i.e. it behaves like persistence; 1/3 are under-predictions (rises) and 2/3 over-predictions (falls).
Among the largest 1 % of absolute errors (68 hours) 100 % occur in rapid-change hours (|change| >= training P90) and 41 % are under-predictions; these hours carry 43 % of the squared error. ================================================
LIMITATIONS
================================================
1. One chronological split and one test window (6,752 hours, 2016-05-12 to 2017-02-28): no variability estimate across other periods.
2. One-step-ahead with the observed current PM2.5: the forecast is dominated by persistence (lag-1 autocorrelation 0.96; the final model explains only 13 % of the variance of the one-hour change); longer horizons were not evaluated.
3. Rows with a missing target, a missing current PM2.5 or an unusable history are excluded (policy documented); results apply to hours with this information available.
4. Heavy-tailed target: squared-error linear models under-react to abrupt episodes; target transforms / quantile models are outside the allowed family.
5. Residuals are heteroscedastic, heavy-tailed and autocorrelated (Durbin-Watson 1.60, Ljung-Box p = 1.1e-53 at lag 24): inference uses HAC / block bootstrap but remains descriptive and post-selection.
6. CV scores of the selected configurations are not nested; thresholds of the hypothesis and selection rules are conventions fixed in advance.
7. DECISION_THRESHOLD = 10 ug/m3 is a PROPOSED operational tolerance, not an authoritative standard - please confirm or override it in src/config.py.
8. A single station (Aotizhongxin); regional transport and boundary-layer variables are not observed. The UCI snippet fetch_ucirepo(id=381) refers to a different dataset; the experiment uses UCI id 501.
9. The linear restriction costs accuracy here: Random Forest on the same engineered features reaches a test MAE of 9.53 (-5.3 % vs the final model) and Gradient Boosting on the same engineered features reaches a test MAE of 9.71 (-3.5 % vs the final model) (reference only - nonlinear models are not allowed as the final model; training-CV MAE: Random Forest 10.45, Gradient Boosting 10.87, final model 10.51).
