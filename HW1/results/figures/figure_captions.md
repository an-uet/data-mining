**figure_03_pm25_temporal_pattern** - Training-only temporal structure of the target (clock of the target hour). (a) Daily cycle: the hourly mean ranges from 76 (16:00) to 93 µg/m³ (0:00), a spread of 20 % of the overall mean; the band is the inter-quartile range. (b) Seasonal cycle: monthly means from 59 (August) to 104 µg/m³ (December); dots are medians. (c) Weekly pattern: weekday mean 81.9 vs weekend 87.0 µg/m³. These patterns motivate the cyclical temporal features (H3).

**figure_04_correlation_heatmap_train** - Training-only Pearson (left) and Spearman (right) correlation matrices of the raw measurements and the target (next-hour PM2.5). Pearson correlation with the target: PM2.5(t) 0.97, PM10 0.84, CO 0.75, NO2 0.67; TEMP and DEWP correlate at 0.81 with each other and PM10 with PM2.5(t) at 0.86.

**figure_05_feature_target_correlation_ranking** - Absolute Pearson and Spearman correlation of every candidate feature with the next-hour PM2.5 (training rows only). The current PM2.5 (Spearman ρ = 0.97), the first lag (0.92) and the short rolling means (ρ ≥ 0.84 for the 3- and 6-hour windows) rank highest; among the other raw measurements PM10 (0.87), CO (0.79) and NO2 (0.69) are strongest. Temperature (+0.00), pressure (-0.12) and rain (-0.04) are only weakly related, and 13 of the 15 wind-direction dummies have |ρ| < 0.10.

**figure_06_tuning_curve** - Mean 5-fold time-series CV MAE of the selected family (Lasso) against the regularisation strength α (band: ±1 standard error). Orange: selected configuration (α = 0.215; 29 of 54 coefficients non-zero; CV-MAE 10.51 µg/m³). For every α up to the selected value the CV MAE is within 0.11 µg/m³ of the minimum; at the largest α of the grid (10) it is 15.48 µg/m³. For Ridge and ElasticNet the CV optimum lies on the no-shrinkage edge of the grid (table_S07): the penalty did not help, i.e. the fit is numerically OLS.

**figure_07_model_comparison** - Baselines and linear models (linear models on Set C): mean 5-fold time-series CV error on the training rows (blue) and one evaluation on the held-out test period (orange); ★ and the grey band mark the selected final model (Lasso). Top row: full scale (the training mean, test MAE 61.9, dominates the axis); bottom row: zoom without the training mean. Test MAE: persistence 10.36, single-feature OLS 10.47, Random Forest 10.20, Gradient Boosting 10.27; the four linear families 10.05-10.06 (final model 10.06, -2.9 % vs persistence; Diebold-Mariano p = 4.4e-05). Test R² is 0.955-0.956 for the linear families and 0.948 for persistence. CV and test errors cover different periods and are not directly comparable; the fold-to-fold standard deviation of the final model's CV MAE is ±1.29.

**figure_08_feature_ablation** - Controlled feature ablation with one linear family (Lasso); folds, grids and the selection procedure are identical, only the feature set changes. (a) CV MAE (blue, ±1 standard error) and test MAE (orange) of every variant. (b) MAE change relative to Set C in % (> 0 = worse than C); the axis is clipped at +5 %, longer bars are labelled. Removing the PM2.5 history (current value, lags, rolling) raises MAE by +97 % (CV) / +61 % (test); removing only lags and rolling terms (keeping PM2.5(t)) +1.4 % / +2.1 %; removing the environment +1.5 % / +0.1 %. Removing temporal, lag, rolling, interaction, pollutant, meteorological or derived-difference features singly changes CV MAE by -0.3 to +0.9 % and test MAE by -0.5 to +1.2 %. Relative to Set C: Set A +3.1 % / +3.3 %, Set B +2.4 % / +3.2 %, Set B′ -0.1 % / -0.6 %. Sensitivity run (not a candidate): Set A without PM2.5(t) +127 % / +92 %.

**figure_09_actual_vs_predicted** - Final model (Lasso, Set C) on the held-out test period (6,752 hours; MAE 10.06, R² 0.955). (a) Actual vs predicted hourly PM2.5 (axes clipped to 0-500 µg/m³; 0.3 % of the hours lie outside). (b) Daily means over the 291 test days (correlation 0.999). (c) Ten-day window around the largest absolute error (orange dot; target hour 2017-01-28 01:00, actual 576, predicted 284 µg/m³). The forecast follows the level closely but stays near the last observed value: it explains 13 % of the variance of the one-hour change PM2.5(t+1) - PM2.5(t) (linear Set A 4 %, persistence 0 % by construction), so abrupt rises and falls are reproduced about one hour late.

**figure_10_residual_analysis** - Residual diagnostics of the final model on the test period (residual = actual - predicted). (a) The spread grows with the predicted level: the residual standard deviation in the highest prediction decile is 6.6 times that in the lowest (Breusch-Pagan p = 6.5e-72); orange: mean residual in 20 prediction bins. (b) Monthly mean residuals range from -0.5 to +2.3 µg/m³ (overall +0.89; 54 % of the hours under-predicted), but the swings grow in the pollution season: monthly MAE 6.7 (May 2016) to 15.1 (Dec 2016). (c) Residual distribution on a log density axis: skewness -0.62, excess kurtosis 36, far from normal (Jarque-Bera p < 1e-300); this describes the errors and is neither evidence for nor against the model. (d) Actual vs predicted. (e) Absolute error over time. (f) Residual autocorrelation (lag 1: +0.20, lag 24: +0.02; Durbin-Watson 1.60; Ljung-Box p = 1.1e-53 at lag 24; band: ±1.96/√n), so classical OLS standard errors would be too small - the inference tables use HAC errors and a block bootstrap.

**figure_11_coefficients_confidence_intervals** - Coefficients of the final model (Lasso, Set C) in µg/m³ of next-hour PM2.5 per one standard deviation of the (centred) feature, with 95 % intervals. Blue: penalised predictive model with moving-block-bootstrap intervals (one-week blocks); orange: OLS companion on the same standardised design with HAC (Newey-West) intervals. 29 of the 54 coefficients are non-zero; 20 bootstrap intervals exclude zero and 22 OLS-HAC p-values stay below 0.05 after Holm correction. Largest effects: PM25_t (+79.7), PM25_lag_1 (-8.1), WSPM_x_PM25 (-3.3). The three panels have different x-scales. The current PM2.5 and its first lag are strongly collinear (VIF 25 and 130) and have opposite signs, so they must be read together: the pair acts as the current level plus a fraction of the last one-hour change (momentum), not as two independent effects.

**figure_12_slice_performance** - Test-set MAE (left) and RMSE (right) of the final model (Set C), the same linear family on the raw features (Set A) and persistence, by slice (17 slices in 6 groups; slices of different groups overlap). Cut-offs come from the training data or the calendar. The PM2.5-level and one-hour-change slices are defined by the realised outcome: this favours persistence in stable hours and penalises every model in rapid-change hours, so they show where errors occur, not a condition known in advance. Largest final-model MAE: rapid rise (>= +25) (44.3); rapid fall (<= -25) (43.6); episode (>= P95 = 247) (26.5); smallest: stable (|change| <= 6) (4.1). The final model has a lower MAE than persistence in 14 of 17 slices and than Set A in 15; it is worse than persistence in: low (<= P25 = 23), episode (>= P95 = 247), stable (|change| <= 6).

**figure_S02_target_vs_pollutants_meteorology** - Next-hour PM2.5 against PM10, NO2, CO, O3, temperature and dew point (training rows; y clipped at 500 µg/m³). **figure_S03_wind_effects** - Training-only wind effects. (a) Median next-hour PM2.5 by wind direction: lowest for NNW (15) and NW (15), highest for SSE (84) µg/m³; direction matters, hence one-hot encoding without an artificial ordering. (b) Mean and median PM2.5 by wind speed at t: the mean is highest in the 0.5-1 m/s bin (106) and lowest in the 6-20 m/s bin (22 µg/m³), i.e. ventilation lowers PM2.5, which motivates the wind x PM interactions.

**figure_S04_missing_values_policy** - Missing-value policy (training rows). (a) The PM2.5 series has 162 gaps; 87 % last at most 3 hours, the longest lasts 343 hours. (b) Causally forward-filling the PM2.5 history over gaps of up to 3 hours keeps 92.7 % of the training hours (no filling: 81.7 %); a 12-hour limit would add only 1.4 points and a 24-hour limit 2.2. The target is never filled; rows with unusable history are excluded identically for every model.

**figure_S05_feature_redundancy** - Redundancy of the Set C training design (54 columns). (a) Pearson correlation matrix ordered by hierarchical clustering: the PM2.5 history (current value, lags, rolling means) forms one tightly correlated block that is also related to PM10, NO2 and CO. (b) The 22 columns with the highest variance-inflation factor (log axis): 10 columns exceed VIF = 10 (maximum 377 for PM25_roll_mean_3); the 10 largest are PM25_roll_mean_3, PM25_roll_mean_6, PM25_lag_1, PM25_lag_3, PM25_roll_mean_12, PM25_lag_6, PM25_roll_mean_24, PM25_t, TEMP, PM25_lag_12. Such collinearity makes individual history coefficients unstable and motivates penalised regression.

**figure_S06_autocorrelation** - Autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation of hourly PM2.5 (training rows). The autocorrelation falls from 0.96 at lag 1 to 0.38 at 24 h and -0.003 at one week. The partial autocorrelation is dominated by lag 1 (0.97) with a negative lag-2 term (-0.20); beyond lag 2 no partial autocorrelation exceeds 0.04 in absolute value (lag 24: -0.026, lag 168: -0.002), so the daily and weekly lags are expected to add little once the most recent hours are known.

**figure_S07_decision_threshold_sensitivity** - Share of test predictions whose absolute error is at most τ, for several tolerances τ (µg/m³). At the proposed τ = 10 µg/m³ the final model is within tolerance in 70.0 % of the hours (linear Set A 69.6 %, persistence 71.1 %, training mean 8.9 %). The tolerance is a configurable, non-authoritative operating choice: the proposed default is marked and the curves show how the comparison depends on it.
